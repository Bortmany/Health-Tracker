# Cut — Competitor Analysis
**Date:** 2026-09-01 · **Market:** Global, English · **Scope:** Workout-plan apps + fat-loss/macro trackers (Cut spans both)

## 1. What Cut actually ships today (from README.md / CLAUDE.md, not backlog)

- Onboarding quiz (age, experience, goal, equipment, days/week) → matched to 1 of 14 pre-built plans (calisthenics, powerlifting, muscle building, cardio, general fitness, over-50 joint-friendly), with progression rules and deload weeks built in
- Free tier = 4-week plans; Premium = 52-week periodized plans
- Daily log: weight, waist, sleep, HRV, recovery, strain, steps, habits, activities, injury check-ins
- Nutrition: calories, macros, meals
- Training log: rest timer, 50-exercise library with form cues + autocomplete, personal records, "last time" hints per exercise
- Dashboard: weight trend chart, weekly habit ring, logging streak; Progress page with charts and PRs
- Coach accounts: invite codes (redeem = consent), client weight/session summaries, assign/edit programs directly in a client's account, tagged "From your coach"
- Installable PWA; `POST /api/health-sync` ready for future native apps (device data fills blanks, never overwrites manual entries)
- Dormant switches (code shipped, not live): AI-written plans (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), paid Premium upgrades (needs Stripe env vars) — **no public price is set yet**

Cut is unusual in the market: it is a *single app* that does quiz-matched structured programming, daily fat-loss tracking (weight/sleep/steps/habits), macro/meal logging, **and** a lightweight coach layer, all in one login. Every competitor below does at most two of those three things.

**Reading the table:** the workout-tracker side has cratered to $2–5/month (Hevy, Strong) with generous free tiers, because logging itself is now a commodity — the money is in *programming intelligence* (Fitbod, Caliber, Juggernaut AI) or in *coaching* (Caliber). MacroFactor charges a premium ($6–12/mo) but has no free tier and is well-regarded for it — algorithm quality, not price, is its moat.

## 4. User sentiment (Reddit, app-store reviews, review-aggregator sites; patterns only, one-offs excluded)

- Praise: long-term users (1+ year) rate it near-universally 4–5★ and call it habit-changing; the AI genuinely personalizes once it has ~10–15 logged workouts. - Complaint (repeated): price ("a little expensive for what it is") and billing/subscription friction via the App Store. [Aggregated] ([justuseapp.com](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1041517543/fitbod-workout-fitness-plans/reviews))

**Hevy**
- Praise (repeated, strongest of any app researched): "best workout tracker I've ever used… simple, free, tons of graphs"; the free tier is called out specifically as unusually generous for a fitness app. ([sensai.fit](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-review-2026), [setgraph.app](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-workout-tracker-app-reddit))

[Aggregated] ([coachway.io](https://coachway.io/articles/hevy-coach-review/))

**Strong**
- Praise: default recommendation in r/weightroom/r/powerlifting for pure logging speed — "no social feed, no AI suggestions, no bloat, open it, log a set, close it." [Aggregated] ([setgraph.app](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-workout-tracker-app-reddit))
- Complaint: free tier capped at 3 custom routines, restrictive for anyone who program-hops; deeper volume/muscle-group analytics gated behind Premium. [Aggregated] ([setgraph.app](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-workout-tracker-app-reddit), [garagegymreviews.com](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/juggernautai-review))

- Praise: the 1:1 coaching is repeatedly called "worth it," with one reviewer justifying an $800 spend on the strength of coach quality. ([techsith.com](https://techsith.com/how-much-does-the-caliber-app-cost/), [aol.com](https://www.aol.com/articles/elite-fitness-app-wants-set-180732283.html))

- Complaint (minor, UX): can only send one progress photo at a time to a coach, no caption. [Aggregated]

**Boostcamp**
- Praise: full 11,000+ program library, including 130+ coach-designed programs, entirely free — repeatedly cited as the best free option. ([boostcamp.app](https://www.boostcamp.app/premium))
- Complaint (repeated, recent): app stability — crashes, slow loads, workout completion not registering, rest-timer audio bugs, web/mobile sync loses data, workouts skip when the screen locks mid-session. [Aggregated] ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/boostcamp-gym-workout-fitness/1529354455))

## 6. Pricing recommendation

No competitor in this space **combines** structured periodized programming + full nutrition tracking + coach mode in one subscription, so there's no exact analog to price against — but the two halves bracket it clearly:

- Workout-plan-only apps with real programming intelligence (not just logging) cluster at **$8–16/mo** (Fitbod $8–16, Juggernaut AI $30–35, Caliber Plus $12) or **$60–96/yr**.
- Macro trackers with a real free tier but paid unlock cluster at **$7–20/mo** or **$60–80/yr** (MyFitnessPal, Lose It) — and are the ones drawing the most price-resentment sentiment at the top of that range.
- MacroFactor, the best-regarded "does one thing very well" app in this set, sits at **$6–12/mo / ~$72/yr** with *no* free tier and strong sentiment specifically because the price maps to a clear quality bar.

**Recommendation: Premium at $9.99/month or $69.99/year (~$5.83/mo, a ~42% annual discount).**

Justification: this sits just above Fitbod's annual effective rate (~$8/mo) and just below MacroFactor's (~$6/mo) — reasonable given Premium bundles what Fitbod does (52-week periodized programming) *and* what MacroFactor does (macro tracking) *and* coach-assignable programs, which nothing in the researched set combines at a single price. It stays well under Caliber's $150+/mo coaching tiers (Cut's coach mode is lighter-touch, not 1:1 coaching, so it shouldn't be priced like it) and under Lose It's newly-unpopular $79.99/yr-with-no-monthly-option model — landing Cut in the price band sentiment treats as fair (Fitbod, Boostcamp Pro, MacroFactor) rather than the band drawing backlash (MyFitnessPal Premium+, Lose It 2026 repricing). A lifetime SKU (~$149–199, following Hevy/Strong/Lose It precedent) is worth adding once billing is wired up — it's repeatedly cited as the best-value pick in reviews and costs little to implement once Stripe Checkout exists.
