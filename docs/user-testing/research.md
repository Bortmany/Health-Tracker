# Cut — who the users are and what they will want (2026-09-07)

The single most important finding: Cut's core bet — one app that matches an unsure beginner to a structured plan and then tracks everything (weight, sleep, nutrition, training, coach) in one place — lines up with real, well-documented anxieties (gym intimidation, decision fatigue, shame at red numbers) and with a genuine gap in the market (no researched competitor does more than two of quiz-matched programming / daily tracking / coach tools at once [1]), but that "breadth wins" bet has never been tested against an actual user choosing Cut over stacking two or three specialist apps — it rests on a repo synthesis with no source behind it [2]. Of the roughly 120 claims behind this document, about a quarter (31) are high-confidence — almost all of them either directly-fetched clinical literature on weighing anxiety ([3], [4]) or claims read straight out of Cut's own repo docs at high confidence but never checked against a real user. Two-thirds (83) are medium-confidence search-engine snippets — directionally useful but never independently verified, because every app-store review page, Reddit thread, and vendor pricing page was blocked from direct fetch in this sandbox — and the remaining 8 are low-confidence guesses (unsourced synthesis or single weak snippets). Read the whole document with that ceiling in mind: it is reasoning built on secondary sources, not a verified user study.

## 1. Who this user really is

### Persona A — "Sam", the unsure beginner (primary, consumer side)
One-line bio: an adult who wants to lose fat, has tried a diet app before and quit, and has no idea what to actually do at the gym.

- **Job hired for:** functional — "tell me exactly what to do so I stop guessing"; emotional — "don't make me feel judged or stupid while I figure this out"; social — mostly private, low desire to be seen struggling.
- **Anxieties:** gym intimidation is not a minor worry — 47% of UK adults feel uncomfortable at the thought of joining a gym and 32% fear being judged, with "not knowing what you're doing" the single most-cited cause [5]; red/alarm-colored calorie or weight numbers read as shame, not information, per a UCL/Loughborough analysis of 58,881 fitness-app posts that found users feeling ashamed logging "unhealthy" food and one user told to eat "negative 700" calories a day [6]; some in this persona actively don't want to see the raw weight number at all — CBT literature for eating-disorder patients shows "blind weighing" produces less anxiety and more tolerance of uncertainty than seeing the number [3], and anxiety about the social consequences of weight gain is a clinically documented, central feature of disordered eating, not a UX nitpick [4].
- **Trigger that brings them:** decision fatigue, not lack of motivation — "the single biggest barrier for a beginner isn't the lifting, it's not knowing what to train today" [7], echoed by beginner-fitness commentary that most beginners quit from not knowing what to do, not from lack of drive [8].
- **First-session moment that has to happen:** completing one real, matched action — not just seeing a plan summary. Product literature puts the retention inflection point at 2-4 completed workouts, with users who cross it retaining at 2-3x the rate of one-and-done users [9]; the "aha moment" discipline says get the user to that one moment fast rather than touring features [10]. Cut's own quiz is capped at 5 steps for exactly this reason — a 4-step onboarding tour completes ~40.5% of the time but a 5th step drops that to ~21% [11], and the redesign spec explicitly protects the 5-step cap by pointing at Noom's notorious 113-screen funnel as the anti-pattern [12].
- **What makes them come back tomorrow:** week-1 daily-log completion is reported as the strongest single predictor of 90-day retention [13]; a forgiving, adjustable routine (missed-day support, plain-English progress, easy restart) beats forced streaks [14], because rigid streaks make a single missed day feel like erasing months of effort and drive avoidance [15] — streak users who treat it as all-or-nothing are 63% more likely to fully quit after one miss, and gamification's retention edge shrinks sharply after six months as novelty fades, so it can't be the only hook [16].
- **What makes them trust it:** transparent handling of health data before any paywall — privacy/security perception is a consistently negative predictor of health-app adoption when handled badly [17] — and never dressing up automation as a human ("coach" bots are a top Noom complaint [18]; Cut's own docs make AI-generated plans a mandatory-label requirement for exactly this reason [19]).
- **What makes them abandon it:** being shown a target that reads as punishment, not partnership (the UCL "negative 700 calories" case [6]); a broken streak with no forgiveness [15]; ads or paywalls hitting the core logging action (MyFitnessPal's ad load is called "unusable" and is a top 1-star driver [20]); and — for the sizeable and fast-growing slice of this persona now on GLP-1 medication (~11-12% of US adults per two 2025/2026 surveys [21]) — an app that doesn't account for reduced appetite/activity, since GLP-1 users are documented to significantly cut physical activity, not increase it [22].

### Persona B — "Priya", the independent online coach (secondary, coach side)
One-line bio: runs a handful of online coaching clients from her phone, currently juggling WhatsApp threads and a spreadsheet.

- **Job hired for:** functional — one place to assign/edit programs and see who's on track; emotional — look professional to her own clients without paying for that professionalism; social — clients seeing her as organized and responsive.
- **Anxieties:** losing clients to disorganization — coaches evaluating platforms consistently rank centralized client management as the #1 want and complain most about billing accuracy, reliability/crashes, and reporting depth, not about programming sophistication [23]; Trainerize draws exactly these complaints (crashes/data loss in ~75% of its negative reviews, billing errors, a 4-8 hour onboarding curve) [24].
- **Trigger that brings them:** hitting a breaking point managing clients over WhatsApp and spreadsheets, commonly cited (with a commercial-blog bias caveat, see below) around 15-20 clients [25] — though that specific number comes entirely from vendor marketing content and has no independent survey behind it [26].
- **First-session moment that has to happen:** inviting one client and seeing their summary populate cleanly — the single-pane-of-glass view Trainerize users rate 97% positive is the bar to clear [27], with weight-trend direction, sessions-completed, and last-log date visible per row without a tap [28].
- **What makes them come back tomorrow:** visible, trend-over-time proof the plan is working — what actually keeps a client paying a coach is consistent evidence plus someone accountable for adjusting when it isn't, not the software's feature list [29].
- **What makes them trust it:** reliability and billing accuracy over feature sprawl — Trainerize coaches complain about exactly this gap [30], and Cut's flat, bundled coach pricing directly answers Trainerize's add-on-heavy, per-client model ($9-248/mo plus a $169 branded-app add-on and $45/mo nutrition add-on) [31].
- **What makes them abandon it:** a clunky or unreliable mobile experience for assigning programs and replying to clients on the go [24]; a fragile nutrition integration instead of native data ([32]); no way to look "branded" to her own clients, since white-labeling is a named want for this persona [33]; and no way to spot a quietly disengaging client before they churn, since platforms increasingly build "at-risk" flags for exactly this [34].

Where Cut is different:
- No researched competitor bundles quiz-matched programming, full daily logging, nutrition, a training log, and coach tools in one free tier — each specializes in at most two of those lanes [1], though this "breadth is the differentiator" claim is a repo synthesis, not something a user has actually confirmed they want over stacking apps [2].
- Cut's free tier already includes what most of this list paywalls (macro tracking, unlimited logging) — a real, statable advantage against MyFitnessPal's ad load and entry cap [38] and Fitbod's 3-workout trial [55].
- Cut's flat coach pricing (nothing shipped yet, but the intent is one tier, no add-ons) is a direct answer to Trainerize's per-client, add-on-heavy model, which is coaches' most-named platform complaint [68].

## 3. What users say

**Theme: shame and red numbers (strong evidence — clinical + repeated snippets)**
- "[Users reported] shame at logging 'unhealthy' foods... one case was told to eat negative 700 calories a day" — UCL/Loughborough study of 58,881 posts, 2024-2025 [6].
- Noom still labels foods Green/Yellow/Orange by density, raising long-standing shaming concerns even though nothing is technically off-limits — abbylangernutrition.com, undated [45].
- CARROT Hunger's insult-driven "tough love" ("gluttonous humans") drew controversy, and a cited UCL-linked study found weight-based shame does not actually encourage weight loss — Fox News, undated [69].
- Clinically: eating-disorder patients using "blind weighing" report significantly less anxiety than those who see the number — PMC, peer-reviewed [3].

**Theme: cancellation and billing friction (strong evidence, regulatory-backed)**
- A MyFitnessPal user "was charged for two years after believing they'd cancelled in 2023" — BBB complaints page [37].
- Another was billed $84.99 on renewal after believing they'd already cancelled, refunded only after following up weeks later — BBB, 2025 [70].
- Noom paid roughly $62M to settle a dark-pattern class action over hard-to-cancel subscriptions — cited in Cut's own competitor doc, July 2026 [46].

**Theme: onboarding length (strong, cross-competitor pattern)**
- MacroFactor's onboarding is "thorough but front-loaded" and can overwhelm casual, nutrition-illiterate users before their first log — outlift.com, 2026 [40].
- Noom's questionnaire runs 15-30 minutes before a plan or price is shown — noom.com, 2026 [43].
- Lose It!'s "simple, goal-driven" flow — set a target, get a budget, start logging — is repeatedly praised in contrast — fitbudd.com, 2026 [66].

**Theme: streaks and loss aversion (medium-strong, behavioral-design literature)**
- "Streak resets... erase months of effort... driving avoidance of the app afterward" — nutrola.app review analysis, 2026 [15].
- Rigid-streak users are "63% more likely to fully quit" after a single missed day (cited CHI 2020 finding) — thedecisionlab.com, 2025 [16].

**Theme: coach pain points — reliability and billing, not features (medium, single-source-heavy)**
- Trainerize draws complaints of "frequent app crashes and data loss (cited in ~75% of negative reviews)," billing errors, and a mobile app that's "clunky for replying to check-ins... on the go" — coachway.io, 2026 [24].
- Coaches rate Trainerize's centralized client management 97% positive — the one thing the incumbent gets right — same source [27].

**Theme: logging friction and food-database quality (medium)**
- MyFitnessPal's food database is "heavily user-submitted," leading to duplicate entries and wrong barcodes — fitia.app, undated [71].
- Carbon Diet Coach, despite a premium price, requires fully manual food entry — "a significant gap" at that price point compared to competitors — nutriscan.app, 2026 [64].

**Theme: "I don't know what to train" (medium, but directly validates Cut's core mechanic)**
- "The single biggest barrier for a beginner isn't the lifting, it's the decision fatigue... what should I train today?" — fitbod.me, 2025-2026 [7].
- Fitbod's AI-generated suggestions are called "generic/repetitive," with some Reddit users saying they'd do better assembling their own plan from free resources — dr-muscle.com, undated [72].

## 4. Local facts that change the design

(Market lens: global English-speaking consumers/coaches, per the brief — no GCC-specific facts were found; that gap is called out explicitly below.)

- **FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 on procedural grounds, but the FTC keeps enforcing via lawsuits (Match, Chegg, Cleo AI, Amazon)** [73], 2025-09. Design consequence: build one-click, in-app cancellation into the Paddle flow regardless — enforcement risk is live, and this exact persona is shame-prone around feeling "trapped."
- **The EU requires a prominent, always-available cancel button, effective June 19, 2026** [74], 2025-11; **UK's own subscription reform is delayed to autumn 2026** [75], 2025-11. Design consequence: since Cut is global/English and will take EU signups, the account/billing screen needs a visible cancel button live before mid-2026, which will likely also satisfy the UK rule when it lands.
- **Apple/Google in-app purchase commission is 30% (15% for small-business program or year-2+ renewals); Paddle instead charges a flat 5%+$0.50/transaction and handles VAT/GST/sales-tax remittance across 100+ jurisdictions as merchant of record** [76] [77], 2025. Design consequence: Cut's PWA + Paddle billing choice avoids a 15-30% platform tax and a solo owner's tax-compliance burden — don't undo this by adding native in-app purchase later without weighing the cost.
- **Native apps still account for ~90% of smartphone time despite PWA growth; Bluetooth/NFC/background-location use cases still favor native** [78], 2025. Design consequence: Cut being PWA-only is a real adoption headwind and rules out (for now) a wearable/rest-timer companion; the health-sync endpoint should keep expecting manually-pushed device data.
- **~11-12% of US adults now report current GLP-1 use for weight loss, roughly double 2024 levels, and GLP-1 users are documented to significantly decrease physical activity, not increase it** [21] [22], 2025-2026. Design consequence: onboarding/quiz logic shouldn't assume calorie-restriction-only fat loss, and the training log could explicitly frame resistance work as "keep your muscle while the medication does the appetite work" for this fast-growing segment.
- **Diet/nutrition apps see roughly 80% abandonment within the first month; week-1 logging frequency is the strongest predictor of 90-day retention** [79] [13], 2026. Design consequence: treat week-1 daily-log completion as its own tracked success metric, and protect it with nudges — not shame — since it predicts whether the free-to-premium window matters at all.
- **A 4-step onboarding tour completes ~40.5% of the time; adding a 5th step drops that to ~21%** [11], undated. Design consequence: Cut's 5-step quiz is already at (or just past) the point where completion craters — resist adding a 6th step for any future feature.

## 5. Feature wishlist, ranked (Kano)

### Must-have (they leave without it)
1. **Keep the quiz to ~5 steps or fewer, no matter what new features get added** — evidence: [11], [43], [40], [12] (4 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: yes, already a 5-step quiz per the redesign spec — protect it, don't extend it.
2. **Never use red/alarm color for over-target numbers or missed logs** — evidence: [6], [45], [69], [80] (4 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: yes, already a stated brand rule in the repo docs — this is a "protect it" item, not a gap.
3. **One-tap, visible in-app cancellation once Paddle billing goes live — no emailing support** — evidence: [37], [70], [46], [73], [74] (5 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: no — Paddle billing is dormant; this needs to be designed in before it activates.
4. **Core daily logging (weight/food/training) must never feel paywalled or ad-interrupted** — evidence: [38], [20], [55] (3 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: check — confirm the free 4-week tier doesn't cap daily-log actions the way MFP's free tier does.
5. **A streak that forgives one missed day instead of resetting to zero** — evidence: [15], [16], [14] (3 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: check — the product description mentions "streaks" with no stated grace mechanism.
6. **Coach program-assignment/messaging must not crash or lose data** — evidence: [24], [23] (2 sources). Persona: Priya. Built: check — no reliability data available for Cut itself; treat as a standing quality bar.

### Expected (they assume it's there)
1. **A weight chart that shows a smoothed trend line, not just raw daily numbers** — evidence: [81], [82], [41] (3 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: in progress — the redesign spec already plans a client-side multi-series LineChart extension.
7. **Fast, low-tap logging with pre-filled previous numbers** — evidence: [83], [52], [49] (3 sources). Persona: Sam. Built: check — confirm Train screen pre-fills the prior session's weights/reps.
8. **Dark-native UI, not a half-finished light/dark toggle** — evidence: [36] (1 source, high-confidence repo claim). Persona: Sam. Built: yes.
9. **A rest timer and exercise-library autocomplete** — evidence: product description confirms this is built; category-standard per [52], [56]. Persona: Sam. Built: yes.
10. **Coach client rows showing weight-trend direction, sessions completed, and last-log date at a glance, in neutral (never red/green) color** — evidence: [28], [84], [85] (3 sources). Persona: Priya. Built: partially — the redesign spec ships an approximated "last log" date, not a true cross-log-type value.
11. **A single, explicit "update just today vs. update the whole program" choice when a coach or user edits a session** — evidence: [86] (1 source, high-confidence repo claim). Persona: both. Built: yes, per the redesign spec.

## 6. What they will ask to change

1. **"Why do I have to type in every single meal — can't it just scan the barcode or a photo?"** — reason: barcode/photo/voice logging is now expected even in premium apps and its absence draws direct complaints ([64], [87]). Suggested response: it's on the roadmap; today's manual entry is deliberately simple so beginners aren't scared off by a data-entry chore, and a curated (not crowdsourced) food list is planned to avoid MyFitnessPal's duplicate-entry problem ([71]).
2. **"Why doesn't my calorie/macro target adjust as I actually lose weight?"** — reason: automatic weekly recalibration from logged data is close to table stakes among nutrition-focused competitors ([41], [63]). Suggested response: this is exactly what the (currently dormant) AI plan writer is built for; it will be clearly labeled as AI-driven, not a human coach, when it's switched on ([19]).
3. **"Why do I have to see the exact number every day — can I just see if I'm trending down?"** — reason: raw daily numbers are a documented anxiety trigger for exactly this persona ([3], [6]). Suggested response: add a "hide the number, show the trend" toggle alongside the smoothed trend chart already planned ([81]).
4. **"Why did my streak reset to zero just because I missed one day?"** — reason: rigid streaks are the single most-cited cause of habit-tracker abandonment ([16], [15]). Suggested response: add a streak-freeze/grace day so one miss doesn't erase the whole run.
5. **"Why can't I log my workout at the gym without wifi?"** — reason: Hevy — the category's most-loved logger — still requires an online connection and draws this exact complaint ([48]); Cut is PWA-only, which structurally leans the same way ([78]). Suggested response: acknowledge this as a known limitation of the current PWA architecture; local-first offline logging with background sync is the fix, not yet built.
6. **"Why doesn't the app know I have a bad knee and stop suggesting squats?"** — reason: Cut already collects injury data in daily logs but doesn't appear to use it in training, which is precisely the gap Fitbod is criticized for leaving open ([89]). Suggested response: agreed gap — surfacing already-collected injury data as exercise exclusions is a near-term, low-new-data-cost improvement.
7. **"Why can't I cancel my subscription without emailing someone?"** — reason: MyFitnessPal's opaque cancellation and Noom's $62M dark-pattern settlement are the two most cited billing complaints in this research ([37], [46]). Suggested response: one-tap in-app cancellation is a hard requirement before Paddle billing activates, not a nice-to-have — and it's also a looming EU legal requirement ([74]).
8. **"Why doesn't my 'done' checkmark stay checked when I refresh mid-workout?"** — reason: this is an explicit, acknowledged limitation in Cut's own redesign spec, not a rumor — the checkbox state is ephemeral client-side only ([90]). Suggested response: known limitation; your weights/reps do save, only the visual checkmark resets — persisting it needs a small schema change, tracked as a follow-up.
9. **"Why doesn't my coach see that I logged my sleep and steps today, just because I didn't log a weigh-in?"** — reason: also an acknowledged gap — the client-summary endpoint approximates "last log" from weigh-ins/sessions only, not all log types ([85]). Suggested response: known v1 approximation; a true cross-log "last activity" date needs an API extension, tracked as a follow-up.
10. **"Why can't I put my own name/logo on what my clients see?"** — reason: white-labeling is a named want among online coaches evaluating platforms ([33]). Suggested response: acknowledged roadmap item, not urgent for v1.
11. **"Why is there no social feed or way to see my friends' workouts?"** — reason: Hevy's social layer is popular, but shame-prone beginners are also documented to dislike being pushed into community features ([91], [48]). Suggested response: deliberately out of scope — any future social layer should be opt-in and coach-only visible, not a public feed, given this persona's shame sensitivity.
12. **"Why does my workout plan look exactly like last week's — is the app even paying attention?"** — reason: Strong lost its category lead by standing still, and "does the plan actually adapt" is a named complaint about static loggers ([54], [50]). Suggested response: Cut's plans do have written progression rules — the fix here may be surfacing that reasoning in the UI rather than actually changing the underlying logic ([58]).
13. **"Why can't my Apple Watch start the rest timer for me?"** — reason: Hevy and Strong both integrate with smartwatches; Cut is PWA-only ([47], [78]). Suggested response: acknowledged gap tied to the PWA-only architecture decision — no near-term fix without native investment.

## 7. What the repo already believed vs. what we found

**What the docs got right:**
- The no-red, non-shaming brand voice ([80]) is directly validated by independent UCL research on calorie-app shame ([6]) and by clinical literature on weight-related anxiety ([4]) — this is the single best-supported design decision in the whole research base.
- Keeping the quiz at 5 steps ([12]) is backed by independent onboarding-completion data ([11]), not just intuition.
- Dark-native design as an advantage ([36]) matches category sentiment.
- The "AI must be labeled" rule ([19]) anticipates a real, already-realized risk (Noom's bot-coach backlash and settlement, [18], [46]).
- Flat coach pricing as a wedge against Trainerize's add-ons ([68]) is well matched to coaches' actual, stated top complaint about the incumbent ([24]).

**What the docs got wrong or stale:**
- The July 2026 competitor-research.md treats the free/premium plan-length split as settled, while the September report is the one that actually proposes a concrete Premium price ($9.99/mo or $69.99/yr) — the two docs are two months apart, study overlapping-but-different app sets, and shouldn't be read as one consistent position; the price is a recommendation sitting unimplemented, not a decision [92] [93].
- The "Cut does what no competitor does" breadth differentiator, repeated with rising confidence across sweeps, is still an internal synthesis that has never been checked against a real user's stated preference for one app over stacking two or three specialists [2].

**What the docs never considered:**
- GLP-1 medication users as a specific, fast-growing segment with different needs (reduced appetite, reduced activity, muscle-preservation framing) — covered here only as a market-size fact, never as a design question [21] [22].
- Willingness to pay for one bundled app versus stacking specialized subscriptions — the $9.99/mo Premium figure is reverse-engineered from competitor pricing gaps, not from any user's stated price sensitivity [93].
- Boostcamp and similar free, community-program-matching lifting apps — the closest structural analog to Cut's own quiz-to-plan mechanic — were never researched in any sweep.
- MacroFactor's own complaint surface was referenced approvingly (as the adaptive-coaching benchmark) but never actually searched for user complaints — that specific gap-fill attempt failed on search-budget exhaustion [94].
- A GCC/Arabic-market read on any of this was scoped out by product decision (English-only, no RTL, [95]) rather than by evidence — it was attempted once across five research sweeps and returned nothing usable.
